# Tarnoc model v2, built from scratch

**Model:** `models/Tarnoc_v2_2026-09-01.xlsx`
**Built by:** `scripts/build_tarnoc_v2.py` (one script, one pass, no second stage)
**Date:** 2026-09-01

A clean rebuild rather than a patch of the existing workbook. Same business, same
house design, different architecture underneath.

## What is the same

Tab order, naming and visual language follow the existing model exactly: Geist
10pt, white bold on black section bars, pale yellow inputs, blue cross-tab links,
grey ratio rows, lighter grey check rows, parenthesised negatives with a dash for
nil, an Arial 9 italic grey notes column on the right of every tab, gridlines off,
frozen panes on all five time series. Tabs: Cover, How to read me, Assumptions,
Financial Statements, Revenue Forecast, COGS, OPEX, Personnel, Dashboard.

Scope is the same too: TTK and Combi+, the upsell basket, installation as a
pass-through, service contracts on the installed base, volume-tiered bill of
materials with the tier basis switch, the case switch, and full three statements
with a balance sheet check.

## What is different

**Monthly the whole way, Jan-2026 to Dec-2030.** Sixty real columns, no
annual-only columns anywhere. Most of the bugs in the old file lived in the
annual 2029 column: hardcoded inventory, hardcoded interest, a capex line that
did not exist. That failure mode is now structurally impossible.

**Units sold is an output.** Three numbers are computed every month and the
smallest wins:

1. Orders the funnel generates: marketing spend / cost per lead, through two
   conversion rates
2. What we can sell: our reps x quota, plus productive installer partners x units
   each, capped by the direct and channel mix
3. What we can build: the assembly partner, plus any in-house line producing

Revenue Forecast row 35 names which of the three bit, in every month. The
Dashboard shows it by year.

**No market size or market share anywhere.** The funnel is the demand driver.
EUR120 per lead at a 20% close rate is EUR600 of marketing per customer, which is
what the pricing already assumed, so the funnel is a bridge to a number that was
already in the model rather than a new invention.

**Headcount is driven, and it sits in Personnel.** Ten teams, each sized by
whatever creates its work: support by installed base, buyers by units, trainers by
partners signed, operators per live line, finance and IT at one per nine staff.
The founding team and the certification team are fixed inputs, because they do not
scale with volume.

**2026 is not remodelled, it is carried over.** January to October is the existing
committed plan, held fixed line by line: people and non-people cost for each of
the three departments, taken straight from the current model and not recalculated.
Those cells are flagged as inputs on the OPEX tab. The drivers take over from
November, the month after the money lands. The freeze date is an input, so it can
be moved.

**Three gates hold the plan to reality.** The committed plan holds to October 2026.
Nothing is sold before the first sellable month, set to January 2027. Nobody is
hired before November 2026. All three are dated inputs.

**Capacity and capex follow the handover.** The assembly partner carries the ramp.
In-house lines come live on dates you set, and each is paid for twelve months
before it can build anything. Base case dates sit in 2035, meaning never.

**Warranty reserve sits in COGS**, at EUR200 a unit on top of the yield allowance
already inside the BOM, so it hits gross margin rather than hiding in overheads.

There is a reconciliation on the Dashboard that ties to the euro. The low point of
cash after the raise is **EUR873,740 in January 2028**, and the money that gets
there looks like this:

| **Total** | **EUR11,453,120** |

Capex is EUR6.0m across the plan: EUR3.5m in July 2027 for the first assembly line
plus tooling, and EUR2.5m in July 2028 for the second. Each is paid twelve months
before the line can produce anything. Only the first line has been paid for by the
time cash bottoms out, which is why EUR3.5m rather than EUR6.0m appears above.

**The plan draws down EUR9,129,572 of the EUR10m, or 91%.** The first version of
this build only used EUR8.44m, leaving EUR1.56m idle. Selling capacity, not plant
or demand, was what limited the plan in 34 of 60 months, so the remaining money
went into the channel rather than more capex: marketing up from EUR66k to EUR100k a
month in 2027, reps from 0.75 to 1.25 a month, installer partners from 2.0 to 3.5 a
month. That lifted 2030 volume from 22,475 to 27,965 units and 2029 EBITDA from
EUR56.3m to EUR81.4m, all inside the existing 36,000 unit build capacity.

## How the model was checked

`python3 scripts/audit_v2.py models/Tarnoc_v2_2026-09-01.xlsx`

Four phases, all against a real LibreOffice recalculation rather than openpyxl's
view of the formulas.

**1. Errors and the balance sheet.** Every combination of the two switches is
recalculated and swept for formula errors, and the check row is tested in all 65
columns. Zero errors, ties to 0.0000 in all four combinations.

**2. An independent shadow model.** The whole model is reimplemented from scratch
in Python, straight from the assumption values, and compared to the workbook cell
by cell: 118 rows by 60 months by two cases, **14,160 cells, zero mismatches**.
This is what catches a formula pointing at the wrong row, an off-by-one range, or
a stock that fails to roll forward.

**3. Identities, signs and operating logic.** Sixty-eight checks a shadow model
cannot catch, because a shadow can share the same wrong intent:

* cash articulates (movement equals operations plus investing plus financing,
  closing equals opening plus movement, opening equals prior closing)
* the balance sheet composes correctly at every subtotal, and cash on the balance
  sheet equals cash from the cash flow
* retained earnings equal cumulative net income; share capital equals opening
  equity plus cumulative raises; gross book value equals cumulative capex;
  accumulated depreciation never exceeds it; the loan balance equals cumulative
  drawdowns
* signs: revenue positive; cost of sales, all three operating expense lines,
  depreciation, interest and tax all negative; tax never a credit; receivables,
  inventory, payables and losses carried forward never negative; the capex line
  in the cash flow negative while the capex schedule itself is positive
* logic: units never exceed demand, selling capacity or build capacity; nil units
  before the first sellable month; no hiring before the hiring date; installation
  revenue exactly equals installation cost; the installed base never falls; no
  negative headcount on any of the eighteen team rows
* the ten frozen 2026 months equal the existing plan by department
* every annual column matches its stated basis, whether that is a twelve month
  sum, the December figure, an average or a minimum
* the Dashboard ties to its source sheets, and sources equal uses

**4. Structure.** No hardcoded numbers anywhere in the monthly grid except the
frozen 2026 block. Every cross-sheet reference lands on a labelled row, and the
full reference map is printed for review. Every live cell on Assumptions reads the
Base and Aggressive cells on its own row. No orphan rows. Units and number formats
agree.

The audit is a gate, not a report. The capex sign error would have failed phase 3
on the sources and uses tie, and the two things it flagged on first run turned out
to be faults in the checker rather than the model: Python rounds 0.5 to even while
Excel rounds it away from zero, and the date header row is deliberately unlabelled.
Both were corrected in the checker, not worked around in the model.

## Three things to look at

**Only 0.6 months of cover at the low point.** Deploying 91% of the raise is the
same thing as running with almost no buffer. At January 2028 there is EUR874k in the
bank against roughly EUR1.4m a month of operating cost. A plan this size normally
wants three months. The honest options are to raise more than EUR10m, to slow the
2027 hiring, or to accept the risk knowingly. It should be a decision rather than
an accident.

**The base case runs out of money in 2028.** Cash reaches minus EUR889,376 in
December 2028 and only recovers in 2030. It draws down 130% of a EUR3m raise, which
is the model saying plainly that EUR3m does not fund this plan.

**Revenue per person stays high.** About EUR1.03m in 2029 in the aggressive case,
against Viessmann at about EUR276k and Vaillant at about EUR200k. Some of the gap
is real, because components are bought in and installation is passed through. It is
still worth deciding whether the organisation ratios are right before this goes out.
